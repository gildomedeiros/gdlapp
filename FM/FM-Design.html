Surfer GPS patterns and pan response

Same and opposite describe the paddle relative to the eventual ride. FM determines pan direction from changes in bearing as seen from the mount. Jitter means unwanted back-and-forth camera pan.

Pan can stop mid-ride when it catches up. FM sends pan Stop on the first encoder reading at or past the latest published target. If GP publishes a farther target in the same direction before pan reaches the current one, the same move continues. A restart requires pan to report stopped and a published target ahead in the commanded direction. If pan coasts past that target, FM waits for a farther one.

Live Tracking takeoff findings

Where to put the mount for paddle-out and takeoff

Green paths · good for early pan. Imagine pointing a finger from the mount at each green dot in turn. On the left your finger turns clockwise. On the right it turns anticlockwise. That turn between GPS fixes is what the GPS Data Producer (GP) measures. A steady turn gives GP one direction to publish, so Live Tracking can start pan when the target is ahead in that direction.

Along a blue line · risk of missing takeoff. A surfer paddling straight away gets farther from the mount while their bearing barely changes. If they stay centred, holding pan is fine. If they drift sideways, small GPS variations can alternate left and right. While GP is Moving, Rule 1 needs three consecutive changes the new way to reverse its published direction. An opposite change or a repeat resets the count. If the target is clockwise of the camera while GP still says anticlockwise, Live Tracking keeps pan idle. The surfer can leave the frame before takeoff.

Crossing a blue line is fine. The difficult geometry is travelling along it. Moving the mount along the beach changes the bearings GP sees. Turning the camera on the same tripod changes the starting frame.

Field check · fixes #6183–#6220. From the recorded mount position, the 37 intervals between raw fixes gave 19 clockwise, 13 anticlockwise and 5 repeats. At #6183 the surfer was already about 30 m to the side of the camera centre line, outside the estimated half-width of 20 m. GP still published anticlockwise. It confirmed clockwise at #6220, and the first encoder movement followed 0.52 s later, about 20 s after #6183. 28 November tracking log

Same GPS fixes, different mount position. Recalculating the bearings from 100 m west and 25 m north of the recorded mount gave 32 clockwise, 0 anticlockwise and 5 repeats. The offset-site figures are calculated bearings. The pan timing and estimated frame width above are from the original run.

GP cache and LT handoff

  • At session start the published cache is null. The one-off startup aim can use a fresh in-range position while the cache is empty. After that aim, LT displays Waiting for GP target until GP publishes coordinates with a direction. The operator’s Stop control remains available.
  • Use one snapshot containing latitude, longitude, source packet, position revision, position timestamp, freshness timestamp, state and clockwise or counterclockwise direction. Compute age from the freshness timestamp using the monotonic clock. Rule 0 can refresh freshness with the same coordinates, direction and revision. A state update alone also retains the revision.
  • Keep the latest raw receipt time separate from the published target’s age. Use the configured GPS age limit to determine whether a target can start a new move. Keep the last published direction during Stationary or a GPS gap. The active motor can finish toward its last target.
  • Compare bearings around the mount through north wrap. Use the first trusted fix as the initial comparison point for Rule 1. Compare each newer trusted bearing with its predecessor. A GPS gap clears pending votes before processing the next fix. A fix with unchanged bearing clears the streak. A bearing change in the already published direction clears a pending reversal. For a Stationary departure, use the saved stationary reference. A position publication resets the Rule 0 hold count.
  • Test input. For GP Simulator, feed the pad fixes directly into GP. In LoRa mode, the clockwise and counterclockwise test offsets can adjust receiver positions during Live Tracking. Keep the original GPS coordinates and mark the source. Rule 0 compares successive inputs before filtering.
  • Direction rule review. Rule 1 covers startup when there is no Stationary reference and reversal during movement. Rule 3 covers a departure from Stationary more than 1 m from the reference, including a departure before the first publication. Keep the one-fix departure and three-fix confirmation visible for review against the paddling-into-a-wave risk.

Live Tracking · Two reasons FM stops an active pan while the surfer is in tracking range

  1. Pan reaches the latest published target. FM sends :K1 (request pan Stop) on the first valid encoder reading at or past that target. Pan can catch up between GPS publications, or GP can publish a closer target. In the manual test, GPS #2286 moved the target from 12 m to 10 m while the published direction stayed clockwise.
  2. GP publishes a reversal while pan is moving. FM requests Stop when the published direction is opposite the current slew. The raw reversal must pass GP’s direction rules. FM waits for pan to report stopped before starting in the new direction.

Inside the minimum surfer range, pause motion. The startup aim uses its locked target until crossing. A user Stop waits for both motors.

Live Tracking pseudocode · FM 0.1.94

Live Tracking contract. LT gets all tracking targets and their direction from the GPS Data Producer (GP). Ask the user before changing Live Tracking or the GP-to-LT contract.

Selective pan lead. Arm the configured lead when GP is Moving and Rule 1 publishes in the established direction. The last two consecutive GPS displacements must agree with that direction, and the latest must advance more than 1 m sideways around the mount. Place the pan aim the fixed lead distance ahead along the same range. Keep the lead through same-direction slowdowns. A confirmed reversal or entry to Stationary clears lead for future publications. A held or freshness-only fix keeps the current aim. The first eligible fix out of Stationary can publish immediately with zero lead.

Simulator framing. During continuous Mode 5 (Takeoff), compare the existing pan encoder samples with the latest fresh simulated surfer positions before filtering and lead. Use half the configured shot width on either side of centre. Estimate time outside the frame across observed encoder intervals and report observation coverage against the full takeoff window. Manual or CSV fixes carry no continuous takeoff label. Compare identical runs with 0 m and 5 m of lead at the same report width.

Live pan speed multiplier. Set a positive multiplier in Settings before starting Live Tracking. The default is 1.0. The first pan always starts at 800×. LT applies the multiplier once when restarting after an acknowledged tracking Stop and confirmed standstill. Each restart uses the latest surfer-to-mount distance before lead. At 200 m, LT calculates about 171×. With a multiplier of 1.3 it commands about 223×. The motor keeps that rate through the slew. A user Stop ends the session.

Matching speed to footage. Each timestamped live_pan_start_sent row includes the GPS fix ID (packet), calculated speed, applied multiplier and commanded speed in sidereal units. The preceding live_pan_speed_selected row includes surfer range, Stop reason, previous rate, motor period and whether the scaled rate hit a limit. Encoder samples show the resulting motion.

The main screen shows flags for Hotspot Wi-Fi, GPS, GTi and calibration, followed by one row for Canon link, recording, Canon events and zoom.
